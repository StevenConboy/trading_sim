# Forward Tests Humble AI Trading Bots

Almost none of the trading bots that fill GitHub, including the Claude "trader skill" repos with thousands of stars, publish a forward track record, and the AI and LLM traders that have been forward tested with real, checkable money mostly lost it. The best-verified test, Nof1's Alpha Arena, ran frontier LLMs from public Hyperliquid wallets: **31 of 38 accounts of $10,000 lost money, the pooled $380,000 fell about 27% in roughly four weeks of trading, fees consumed about $43,000, and the first season's winner lost about 30% in the next** ([TradingAgentStack](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/)). The longest public AI record points the same way: the AIEQ ETF roughly doubled from its 2017 launch while the S&P 500 roughly tripled ([Yahoo/24/7 Wall St](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ai-help-beat-stock-market-152310707.html)), and all 11 fully AI-run funds in a peer-reviewed census trailed the index ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ai-makes-unreliable-investment-decisions/)). The forward records that do hold up, namely Rob Carver's 12 years of self-reported live futures trading with his open-source pysystemtrade, Numerai's crowdsourced hedge fund, and Hyperliquid's own market-making vault, come from trend following, huge forward-scored ensembles, or the house's order flow, not from retail LLM bots. Verification is the crux: most "verified" badges prove that trades happened, not that the account is real money, representative, or the provider's only one, and backtests are close to worthless as evidence, especially for LLMs that may have memorized the period being tested. For trading_sim, the forward-only, coin-controlled design is already more rigorous than nearly every public LLM trading claim, but one $100 lottery-ticket trade a day needs years to separate skill from luck (about 617 trading days to detect 55% directional accuracy), its four days so far prove nothing, and its ledger currently carries more booking uncertainty than signal.

## Popular trading repos publish stars and backtests, not forward P&L

jakeschincariol/trader-skill fits the pattern exactly: about 2,000 stars and 431 forks, a single commit (October 14, 2026), and no backtest, paper or live results of any kind, with its own README warning that "copying an investor does not copy their results" ([GitHub](https://github.com/jakeschincariol/trader-skill)). The same holds for every high-profile trading repo examined. virattt/ai-hedge-fund, with roughly 45,600 to 59,600 stars depending on the snapshot ([Reskilll](https://blogs.reskilll.com/ai-hedge-fund-the-50k-star-open-source-multi-agent-investment-system/); [SourcePulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1797841)), calls itself a proof of concept "for educational and research purposes" and ships with live trading **"disabled by default"** ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/virattt/ai-hedge-fund/HEAD/README.md)). No forward results surfaced for TauricResearch/TradingAgents either ([Pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_ai_trading_agents/)). The execution frameworks are no different. Freqtrade's maintainers publish no strategy record, and their documentation warns that **"most public strategies are not good performers"** and that **"only forward-testing (dry-mode) can really confirm a strategy"** ([Freqtrade strategy docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/strategy-101/); [Freqtrade backtesting docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/backtesting/)). The community's attempt to fill that gap, strat.ninja, ranked dry runs from statistics that users uploaded from their own bots after as few as two closed trades, with no independent check ([stratninja on GitHub](https://github.com/Bloodhunter4rc/stratninja)), and when checked for this report its domain redirected to a parking page. The Hummingbot Foundation reports trading volume but says it "can't inspect the trading performance of our users directly" ([Hummingbot](https://hummingbot.org/blog/does-community-based-market-making-work/)). QuantConnect's Quant League, the one framework-linked forward scoreboard, ranked live out-of-sample results a quarter at a time, published only the top three (the Q1 2025 winner made 14.88%), and ended with Q4 2025 ([QuantConnect forum](https://quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/19032); [QuantConnect announcement](https://www.quantconnect.com/announcements/19630/quant-league-q3-2025-results-a-close-race-between-lake-forest-and-cuhk/)). The lone exception among star-heavy AI repos is HKUDS/AI-Trader (about 22,200 stars), which ran a live benchmark whose results, covered below, were modest and inconsistent ([star-history](https://www.star-history.com/hkuds/ai-trader/)).

Backtests cannot stand in for those missing records because they barely predict what happens next. Across **888 Quantopian algorithms with at least six months of out-of-sample results, backtest Sharpe explained less than 2.5% of out-of-sample Sharpe**, and the more backtests an author ran, the wider the shortfall, which is the signature of overfitting ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies)). Where products went live, the haircut was severe. 215 investment-bank risk-premia indices posted a **median Sharpe of 1.20 in backtest and 0.31 live**, and 30% had a negative live Sharpe ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/)), while indexes tracked by smart-beta ETFs went from about +3% a year market-adjusted before launch to roughly −0.5% to −1% after ([JFQA](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/smart-beta-mirage/35CA1CEA091A485DDA960BB2D9930BC9)). Even published academic anomalies lose 26% of their return out of sample and 58% after publication ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)).

LLM traders add a flaw that makes their backtests worse than useless: the model may already know the answer. Asked about risks in September–November 2019 earnings calls, **LLMs mentioned COVID-19 in 6.8% of outputs**, and they identified firms whose names had been masked about 70% of the time ([FINM 33200 notes on Sarkar & Vafa](https://finm-33200.github.io/discussions/llms_and_lookahead_bias.html)). Lopez-Lira and Tang, whose ChatGPT return-prediction paper is the field's landmark, conclude that an LLM must be tested only on data after its knowledge cutoff ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)), and the 20-year FINSABER benchmark found that earlier reported LLM trading advantages "deteriorate significantly" over a broader set of stocks and a longer window, which its authors attribute to survivorship and data-snooping bias ([arXiv 2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078)). A trader skill backtested over its own model's training period therefore says nothing, and a forward test is the only clean evaluation. Before deserving trust, such a repo would need a frozen, disclosed model version and prompt; timestamped forward results, ideally broker-linked or on-chain, spanning several months and more than one market regime; a buy-and-hold comparison on the same universe net of fees and slippage; and several runs to rule out one lucky path. None of the high-star repos examined offers any of these.

Below the top rows, "verified" rarely means what buyers assume. Myfxbook's badges confirm that the history shown matches the broker's records and that the publisher controls the account ([Myfxbook](https://www.myfxbook.com/help/verification)), not that the account is the provider's only one or that its headline "Gain" reflects money made. One real Waka Waka grid-robot account shows a **Myfxbook Gain of +12,335% against an absolute gain of +467% and a 66.5% maximum drawdown**, while another account on the same robot rose about 400% and then lost 98.7% over roughly two days in February 2022 ([Oreshnikbot](https://oreshnikbot.com/waka-waka-ea-myfxbook-analysis/)). MQL5 labels each signal "demo or real" and showcases only the top 1,000 by rating ([MetaTrader 5 Help](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/signals/signal_monitoring)); Collective2 says all of its results must be regarded as hypothetical ([Collective2](https://support.collective2.eu/portal/en/kb/articles/why-hypothetical-results-and-performance)); Darwinex Zero traders run virtual capital ([Darwinex Zero](https://darwinexzero.document360.io/docs/understanding-capital-allocation)); and retail AI-signal vendors such as Tickeron describe their headline figures as "historical backtested or platform-reported" ([Tickeron via WBOC](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/press_releases/tickeron-highlights-ai-trading-agent-performance-across-multiple-market-sectors/article_49b9154d-1dfc-5d43-96cd-06218ad853ed.html)). Copy-trading leaderboards built from real exchange records are gamed by discarding failures: a TraderWagon leader can run six portfolios and close losers at will, the **median portfolio lived 2.9 days**, and most of Bybit's promoted "Gold" portfolios collapsed about six days after promotion ([Kawai et al., CHI 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf)). In eToro's own broker data from 2013, copiers earned −10.98 basis points per trade against +1.15 for traders who did not copy, and a trader's performance on one day carried no information about the next ([arXiv 2507.01817](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01817)). At the extreme, Mirror Trading International presented MetaTrader demo-account balances as its "AI bot" record while taking more than **$1.7 billion in bitcoin from at least 23,000 people** ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)).

The common thread is the denominator. Verification certifies one account, while selection happens across every account, portfolio, prompt and start date a provider tried and then hid. The records at the top of the table are informative precisely because they publish the denominator: Alpha Arena exposed every wallet, Bitquery kept depositors who had exited, and the AI-fund census counted the 31 of 54 funds that had already closed. Real-money contests fail this test even though their money is real, because the World Cup ranks only an entrant's best account and does not publish its field size ([Complete Trader's Edge](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=235280)). Documented paper trading sits in the middle. Run forward, it removes look-ahead and most overfitting, which is why Freqtrade insists on dry runs, but it tests neither execution nor behavior under real stakes, a gap that matters a great deal for trading_sim.

## Real-money LLM traders lost 27% in four weeks, and no winner repeated

Alpha Arena is the closest thing to a controlled real-money test of LLM traders. In Season 1, six frontier models each traded $10,000 of crypto perpetuals on Hyperliquid every few minutes from one shared prompt and the same numeric data ([Moby](https://moby.win/learn/alpha-arena/)). Recomputed from on-chain fills, **Qwen3 Max finished +20.3% and DeepSeek +6.9%, while Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost 29.9%, Grok 4 45.1%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 57.7% and GPT-5 63.4%**, and the winner's realized drawdown of 45.2% was more than twice its final gain ([TradingAgentStack](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/)). Season 1.5 added US-stock perpetuals and ran eight models under four rule sets each, $320,000 in all. The anonymous "Mystery Model," later revealed as Grok 4.20, won with about +13% on-chain (+12.11% officially), but **only 5 of 32 accounts made money and 7 of 8 models lost** ([TradingAgentStack](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/); [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/alpha-arena-leaderboard)). As of November 2026, no Season 2 has launched ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/alpha-arena-leaderboard)).

Three patterns matter more than the rankings. Costs did much of the damage: the roughly $43,000 of fees across both seasons equals about **41% of all net losses**, and in Season 1.5 the high-activity "Situational Awareness" accounts burned about 22% of their capital on fees while the low-activity "Monk Mode" accounts paid about 6% and lost least (computed from [TradingAgentStack](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/) figures). Winners did not persist: Season 1 champion Qwen3 Max lost 29.7% in Season 1.5, and runner-up DeepSeek swung from +6.9% to −32.9%. And the prompt mattered as much as the model, since DeepSeek's four Season 1.5 accounts ranged from **+16.7% to −64.6%** under different rule sets. That last finding bears directly on trader-skill repos, which package instructions for a model and are, in effect, prompts. Claude's own record is inconsistent rather than strong: Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost in both Alpha Arena seasons (−29.9% and −39.1%), while Claude 3.7 Sonnet beat QQQ in AI-Trader's US window (+3.11% against +1.87%) and led LiveTradeBench's Polymarket track (+20.54%) ([arXiv 2512.10971](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1); [arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628)). Nof1 itself warned against naming a best model from one short run ([Moby](https://moby.win/learn/alpha-arena/)), and Bloomberg's review concluded that AI bots in public contests "mostly lose money" and "make wildly different decisions when given identical instructions" ([Bloomberg via Daily Gazette](https://www.dailygazette.com/tribune/ai-bots-auditioning-for-wall-street-trading-are-mostly-losing/article_c6fd5b03-c02a-5382-a3b2-ce63716dc754.html); [X summary](https://x.com/cfromhertz/status/2052001782197293396)).

Academic live benchmarks are genuinely forward in time but short, mostly paper, and run once. LiveTradeBench followed 21 LLMs for 50 trading days in August–October 2025. Every model's stock return was positive (+1.95% to +6.25%), which, with no buy-and-hold baseline reported, cannot be separated from simple market exposure, and the correlation between a model's LMArena score and its return was **0.054**, "virtually no relationship" ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628)). HKUDS's AI-Trader found that 3 of 6 models beat QQQ over five weeks of US trading, none of 6 beat the SSE-50 in China, and 1 of 6 beat its crypto index ([arXiv 2512.10971](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1)). In Agent Market Arena's two months of paper trading, no agent beat simply holding TSLA (+46.88%) ([arXiv 2510.11695](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11695v1)). Windows this short cannot carry the conclusions drawn from them: by Lo's standard-error formula, LiveTradeBench's best stock Sharpe of 2.64 over 50 days carries a standard error of about 2.3, so its 95% interval runs from roughly −1.8 to +7.1 ([Lo 2002](https://traders.studentorg.berkeley.edu/papers/The-Statistics-of-Sharpe-Ratios.pdf)). The strongest academic LLM signal shows what longer windows reveal. The GPT-4 news strategy in Lopez-Lira and Tang saw its **annualized Sharpe fall from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 in early 2024**, and **20 basis points of round-trip costs make it unprofitable**, leaving the edge to market makers ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)).

The public experiments that made headlines look different at the finish line. Nathan Smith's ChatGPT micro-cap portfolio was syndicated at **+23.8% after four weeks** ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/25/08/46783783/redditors-100-chatgpt-day-trading-experiment-trounces-two-benchmark-indexes-with-24-returns-heres-how-it-pulled-it-off)) and ended in December 2025 at **$82.88 from $100, while the S&P 500 turned $100 into $111.68**, after a 50.3% drawdown and with annualized alpha of −34% ([Substack, Week 26](https://nathanbsmith729.substack.com/p/chatgpts-micro-cap-portfolio-week-e93)); its own evaluation notes that no transaction costs or spreads were modeled ([evaluation report](https://github.com/LuckyOne7777/LLM-Trading-Lab/blob/main/Experiments/chatgpt_micro-cap/evaluation/evaluation_report.md)). Finder's ChatGPT "fund" (+57.8% in three years) is hypothetical and benchmarked against a peer group that includes 60% and 80% equity funds ([Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/share-trading/share-trading-research/ai-investing); [Algomodels](https://algomodels.com/chatgpt-the-stock-picker/)). Rallies.ai's ChatGPT +72.4% is paper money and the best of about eight models ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/chatgpt-leads-rallies-ai-stock-market-arena/)). The "Claude Portfolio" has a human making final trade decisions, publishes no drawdown or audit, and is mirrored by about $27 million of subscriber money ([explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/claude-portfolio-autopilot-ai-trading-experiment-august-2026)). Every positive claim fails at least one test: paper money, a human in the loop, a few months in a rising market, or a winner chosen from a field after the fact.

The longest real-money AI records belong to regulated funds, and they lag. AIEQ **gained 100.2% from October 2017 through August 2026 against roughly 199.7% for the S&P 500**, about 8% a year against 13% ([Yahoo/24/7 Wall St](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ai-help-beat-stock-market-152310707.html)). Wyatt and Smith's census of 54 AI-driven ETFs and mutual funds found that **every one of the 11 fully AI funds trailed the S&P 500, averaging −1.8% a year against +7.6%, and 31 of the 54 had closed** ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ai-makes-unreliable-investment-decisions/); [Journal of Investing](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijinvest/34/3/74)). The Eurekahedge AI Hedge Fund Index returned 9.8% a year against 13.7% for the S&P 500 from 2009 to 2024 ([IG](https://www.ig.com/za/prime/insights/articles/has-artificial-intelligences-impact-on-hedge-funds-been-overhype-241121)). Failures get relabeled rather than counted: Qraft's QRFT was liquidated in July 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/exchange-traded-concepts-to-close-and-liquidate-qraft-ai-enhanced-us-large-cap-etf-nyseqrft-302813996.html)), while its momentum fund AMOM was relaunched as the "Defiance LLM Catalyst Momentum ETF," which starts the LLM version's record from zero ([BriefGlance](https://briefglance.com/articles/can-llms-fix-momentum-investing-defiance-bets-on-real-time-ai)). The bright spots, such as High-Flyer's reported +56.6% in 2025 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-12/deepseek-founder-liang-s-funds-surge-57-as-china-quants-boom)) and Bridgewater's machine-learning fund matching its human-run sibling at +8.1% in the first half of 2026 ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/bridgewater-macro-fund-gains-h1-2026/)), are privately reported institutional ML programs, not LLM agents anyone can download.

## Durable live edges belong to trend followers, ensembles and the house

The cleanest large-sample evidence on retail-run strategies comes from Hyperliquid, where every vault lives on-chain. Across 9,478 user-run vaults, **64% of 45,275 depositor wallets were down and depositors had lost $32.3 million all-time** as of November 20, 2026, and vault leaders did worse, losing $49.8 million on their own stakes even after $6.1 million in commissions ([Bitquery](https://www.bitquery.io/investigations/hyperliquid-vaults-depositors)). Leaders take 10% of profits while depositors bear all losses, and in 465 vaults leaders gained about $9.5 million while their depositors lost $6.3 million ([Coindoo](https://coindoo.com/hyperliquid-vaults-why-the-leader-can-win-while-you-lose/)). The exchange's own market-making and liquidation vault, HLP, earned **+$140.7 million for 94,626 depositors, only 9% of whom were down**, and among the 17,929 wallets that used both, 91% were ahead in HLP while 62% were behind in user vaults ([Bitquery](https://www.bitquery.io/investigations/hyperliquid-vaults-depositors)). HLP is open to deposits, which makes it one of the few retail-accessible strategies with a fully verifiable forward record, but it is a crypto venue's own risk book and has taken hits, including about $4 million in a March 2025 whale liquidation ([Incrypted](https://incrypted.com/en/the-secret-of-hyperliquid/)). Market making elsewhere tells the same story of edges that belong to infrastructure: Virtu reported **one losing day in 1,238** from 2009 to 2013 ([SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000104746914002070/a2218589zs-1.htm)), but HFT performance tracks relative latency ([Baron et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:54:y:2019:i:03:p:993-1024_00)), and US HFT revenue fell from about $7.2 billion in 2009 to $1.3 billion in 2014 ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/are-the-glory-days-of-hft-over/)).

Trend following is the systematic family with the longest public live record and the smallest backtest-to-live decay; among the bank risk-premia indices, trend and volatility strategies deteriorated least ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/live-performance-of-alternative-beta-products/)). Its live returns are real but lumpy. The SG Trend Index returned **+27.35% in 2022**, its best year since its 2000 launch ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/flat-december-sees-ctas-complete-record-year-2022/)), compounded at **6.9% a year with a 20.4% maximum drawdown over 2020–2025** ([Aussie Turtles](https://www.aussieturtles.com/battle-of-the-trend-following-indexes-december-2025/)), and stood at +15.80% through September 2026 against +12.75% for the S&P 500 total return ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-september-2026/)) before giving some back, with the SG CTA Index at about +11% year to date on November 23 ([BarclayHedge](https://portal.barclayhedge.com/cgi-bin/indices/displayHfIndex.cgi?indexCat=SG-Prime-Services-Indices&indexName=SG-CTA-Index)). It also suffered a lost decade: a CTA critic's tally puts 2010–2019 returns at roughly 0.5–1.6% a year ([Universa](https://universa.net/UniversaResearch_CTAs_202211.pdf)), against 11.2% a year net in AQR's century-long backtest ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). That gap is the real lesson. A genuine, economically grounded edge can trail its backtest by about ten points a year for a decade, so live index returns of roughly 4–7% a year with 10–20% drawdowns are the honest benchmark for any trend bot.

The open-source author with the most credible live record is Rob Carver, whose pysystemtrade code is public and whose numbered annual reports reached **"year 12" in April 2026**. He self-reports a long-run **Sharpe of 0.80 against 0.52 for Man AHL and 0.54 for the SG CTA index**, publishes bad years with cost breakdowns alongside good ones (−16.3% in the year to April 2025, his worst in futures, then +23.7%), and correlates 0.68 with the SG CTA index, which confirms that he runs trend and carry rather than an exotic edge ([qoppac, year 12](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2026/04/annual-performance-update-year-12.html); [qoppac, year 11](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2025/04/annual-performance-update-returneth.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/robcarver17/pysystemtrade)). The record is consistent and candid but unaudited, with no statements and no disclosed capital. Numerai is the crowdsourcing survivor. Its tournament scores each submitted model only on returns that arrive after submission, over a 20-day window following a 2-day lag ([Numerai docs](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/scoring)), and its fund reported **+25.45% net in 2024 (Sharpe 2.75) after −17% in 2023**, after which JPMorgan committed up to $500 million of capacity ([Numerai](https://blog.numer.ai/jpmorgan-secures-500m-capacity/); [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-hedge-fund-numerai-secures-500m-jpmorgan-allocation/)). Its rivals closed. Quantopian returned investors' money in 2020 after its strategy failed to produce results for at least two years, its CEO calling crowdsourced alpha "a moonshot" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantopian); [Business of Business](https://www.businessofbusiness.com/articles/how-quantopian-died-shut-down-quant-investment-robinhood/)), and QuantConnect's Alpha Streams ceased in February 2022 ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/announcements/15944/pioneering-a-free-market-for-alpha/)).

The base rates any retail bot must beat are grim. SEBI found that **87.7% of individual Indian derivatives traders lost money in FY26**; individuals flagged as algo users lost ₹51,728 crore gross across FY25–FY26 while proprietary and foreign institutional algos earned ₹89,452 crore and ₹44,689 crore, and **99% of institutional trading profits came from algo entities**, though SEBI notes that its algo flag overstates genuine individual algo use ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)). About 92% of Indian individuals' losses came from options, which they overwhelmingly bought rather than sold ([Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/sebi-fo-loss-study-explained-why-9-in-10-retail-traders-lost-91685-crore-in-fy26)), and US retail option buyers lose 5–9% on average around earnings announcements ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement)). Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders predictably beat the market ([Barber et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)), and 97% of Brazilian futures day traders who persisted 300 days lost money ([Chague et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/spawpaper/2019wpecon47.htm)). "Algorithmic" is not itself an edge; the winning algorithms are institutional, cheap to run and fast. The better alternatives therefore depend on the goal. For evidence about whether LLMs can trade, public-ledger arenas and live benchmarks beat any star count. For a codebase with a real forward record, pysystemtrade is the strongest found. For a strategy family with persistent live returns, trend following qualifies if its droughts are accepted. For a third-party-scored forward record of one's own model, Numerai's tournament provides one. And for most money, an S&P 500 index fund returned roughly twice what AIEQ did over nine years.

## trading_sim already out-designs most published tests, but needs years

trading_sim gets the hard parts right. It trades forward only, under written rules, with fixed $100 sizing, and its "coin" arm is a randomized control that none of the public LLM experiments reviewed here used; its morning commits record each direction roughly five and a half hours before the outcome, and its tagged orders can be reconciled with Alpaca's records ([RULES.md](../RULES.md); [README.md](../README.md)). That mirrors the professional sequence of embargo, then paper trading, then small real capital, as López de Prado's strategy life cycle is summarized secondhand ([MLdP pipeline summary](https://tradeandroll.substack.com/p/the-mldp-pipeline-building-production)), and Kevin Davey's described practice of incubating strategies without real money for 6 to 12 months ([Algo Advantage](https://algoadvantage.substack.com/p/036-kevin-davey-part-i-its-all-about)). Run honestly and published in full, it would be a rarer artifact than most star-heavy trading repos.

Four days in, the ledger shows the claude arm at −$260 and the coin arm at +$45 over three closed days ([trades.csv](../trades.csv)), and the booking uncertainty already exceeds the gap. On three of the four days the coin picked the same direction as claude, so both arms held the identical contract and their difference was exactly zero; only November 19 separated them. On that day the coin arm's half-exit was booked at $0.57 against a $0.40 limit, $85 more than a live resting order would normally have received, which is consistent with Alpaca's paper engine matching against the NBBO without queue position while not simulating price improvement ([Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)). The same row books five leftover contracts as worthless even though the script's own close order priced them at a $1.27 bid, about $635, and left that order working with no recorded outcome; paper option expirations and exercises sync only the next day ([Alpaca options docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/options-trading)). Depending on how those two items resolve, that single row is worth somewhere between about +$120 and +$840, a spread more than twice the $305 recorded gap between the arms, and three other rows carry the same "still open after close" warning, though at bids of one to three cents. A next-morning reconciliation against Alpaca's orders and account activities, a final-disposition column, and conservative booking (exits at the limit, never better) are prerequisites for any result.

Paper also flatters this instrument more than most. Alpaca's simulator ignores market impact, queue position, latency slippage and regulatory fees, and it never checks order size against displayed depth ([Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)), which matters most for cheap, wide-spread, deep out-of-the-money 0DTE options. trading_sim also prices every order off the free "indicative" feed, which Alpaca staff call "not real data" that "should not be used for live trading" ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/paper-trading-options-pricing/17795)); real OPRA quotes require the $99-a-month Algo Trader Plus plan ([Alpaca market data docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)). The repeated unfilled closes at the indicative bid fit a mismatch between the quotes trade.py sees and those the paper engine fills against, though Alpaca does not document which options quotes paper uses, so the fix is either an OPRA subscription or logging the full quote (bid, ask, timestamp, feed) behind every order and fill. A smaller point: the 3:15 p.m. close exists to beat Alpaca's same-day liquidation, but Alpaca's FAQ says XSP 0DTE options trade until 4:00 p.m., index options generally fall outside its sell-out logic, and far out-of-the-money longs are not auto-liquidated ([Alpaca 0DTE FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/options-expiration-0-dte-faq)), so the early close is harmless but its stated reason may not hold.

The deeper problem is information rate. A Sharpe ratio estimated from a year of daily returns has a standard error of about 1.0, so a year cannot tell a Sharpe of 0 from 1 ([Lo 2002](https://traders.studentorg.berkeley.edu/papers/The-Statistics-of-Sharpe-Ratios.pdf)), and confirming at 95% that a true Sharpe of 1.0 exceeds zero takes about 2.7 years of daily data, or about 6.2 years for 80% power ([Bailey & López de Prado 2012](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf), applied in the research notes). Buying $0.10 options makes it worse because the payoff is a lottery ticket. In an illustrative case where a 10x winner arrives 11% of the time instead of 10%, a 10% edge per trade, confirming that edge takes **roughly 2,400 trades, close to a decade at one a day** (same method). Directional accuracy is a far more efficient target. The paired P&L comparison loses roughly half of all days, when the coin happens to match claude and both arms hold the same contract, while every day yields a verdict on claude's call:

| True accuracy of claude's calls | Trading days for a one-sided 5% test at 80% power | Calendar time at one call a day |
|---|---|---|
| 52% | 3,863 | ≈ 15 years |
| 53% | 1,716 | ≈ 6.8 years |
| 55% | 617 | ≈ 2.4 years |
| 60% | 153 | ≈ 7 months |
| 65% | 67 | ≈ 3 months |

That points to a concrete protocol. Record XSP's price at entry and at 3:15 plus a direction-correct flag, and pre-register a one-sided binomial test of claude's accuracy against 50% with a fixed sample size and stop date, for example 617 trading days to detect 55%, so that watching the running tally cannot manufacture a false positive. Keep the paired claude-minus-coin P&L, with a bootstrap confidence interval, as the secondary test, and consider logging the opposite-direction contract's quotes each day as a shadow arm so that every day yields a paired comparison. Count every prompt or model change as a new trial, record how the claude arm's direction is generated (model, prompt, data), and keep a running trial count in RULES.md, because the expected best of ten skill-free variants over six months shows an annualized Sharpe near 2.2 from luck alone ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf), applied in the research notes). Make the record tamper-evident. Git author dates can be set to anything ([git-scm](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-commit)), so commit and push each decision before the order goes out, stamp each row with the commit hash it ran under, log execution fixes such as the day-one change to the close logic as dated amendments in RULES.md, anchor commits to Bitcoin with OpenTimestamps ([git-ots](https://pypi.org/project/git-ots/); [Peter Todd](https://petertodd.org/2016/opentimestamps-git-integration)), keep the currently git-ignored trade.log or a hash of it, and export Alpaca's order history periodically.

Paper success should not trigger live trading on its own. Stock-simulator users who did best and then opened real accounts traded more and significantly underperformed with real money ([Anginer et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/hbhfxx/v25y2024i4p436-448.html)). A sensible gate is the pre-registered accuracy test passing at its planned sample size plus a positive after-cost paired P&L whose confidence interval excludes zero under conservative fills; only then should the same $100 trade go live, with live fills compared against paper fills before anything scales. FINRA's retirement of the pattern-day-trader rule on June 4, 2026 means account size no longer blocks a small live account from daily round trips ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/blog/finra-retires-the-pdt-rule-introducing-alpacas-new-intraday-margin-framework/)), so the decision rests entirely on evidence.

## Conclusion

The decisive feature of every credible forward record in this research is a published denominator: every wallet, every exited depositor, every closed fund, every bad year. The decisive weakness of stars, verified badges and leaderboards is that they hide it. That reframes what a trader skill is worth. A prompt is not a strategy until its frozen version has a complete public forward ledger, because the same model under sibling prompts produced opposite results, and the denominator explains why the best LLM trading evidence is negative: the tests that publish everything show losses or index-lagging returns, while every positive claim comes from a setting that lets the winner be picked after the fact.

For trading_sim, the value lies less in whether claude wins than in whether the record is complete enough to believe either way. This research found no study that has live-traded an LLM strategy for more than a year with independent verification, so a pre-registered, reconciled, timestamped claude-versus-coin ledger run for two years or more would partly fill a genuine gap. The base rates make a null result the likeliest outcome, and publishing that null with full logs would be worth more than another repo with thousands of stars and no results. If claude does clear a pre-registered 55–60% accuracy bar, the coin arm is what will make anyone believe it.
